### KR-02 — Indonesian building, accessibility, and transparent-door context

- **Sources:** [PP No. 16 Tahun 2021 — BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/161846/pp-no-16-tahun-2021), [Permen PUPR No. 14/PRT/M/2017 — BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/104477/permen-pupr-no-14prtm2017-tahun-2017), and [official regulation PDF](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/136346/PermenPUPR14-2017.pdf).
- **Grade:** A.
- **Purpose:** Ground Indonesian building-governance, accessibility, and glass-door visibility discussions without inventing a film rule.
- **Summary:** PP 16/2021 is a current building-governance instrument whose implementation also depends on other regulations, national standards, and local rules. Permen PUPR 14/2017 is current and addresses building accessibility; its technical material treats conspicuous visual marking on transparent glass doors as a user-safety concern.
- **Grounded facts:** The BPK records show both instruments as in force. PP 16/2021 is high-level and expressly points to further instruments. Transparent-door marking is a visibility/accessibility function, not proof that a decorative film strengthens glass.
- **Incorporation:** Use for Indonesian context, stakeholder questions, manifestation purpose, accessible wayfinding, and the need to check current project/local rules. Describe marking placement qualitatively unless the current full clause and project requirement are verified.
- **Limits/recheck:** Neither source is a generic product approval for applied film. Verify the current consolidated instrument, exact clause, building use, local authority requirements, and any project accessibility standard under GATE-01.

### KR-03 — Indonesian fire/egress context and listed-assembly boundary

- **Sources:** [Permen PUPR No. 26/PRT/M/2008 — BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/104475/permen-pupr-no-), [official regulation PDF](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/95203/PermenPU26-2008.pdf), and [UL fire-rated doors, windows, and hardware guide](https://www.ul.com/thecodeauthority/knowledge/ul-fire-rated-doors-guide).
- **Grade:** A for the Indonesian regulation record; C for UL system guidance.
- **Purpose:** Prevent casual film application to exit doors, fire doors, or fire-rated glazing.
- **Summary:** Indonesian fire-protection requirements create a project-specific fire/egress review context. UL's guide illustrates the broader technical principle that a fire-door opening is an assembly whose glazing, frame, hardware, labels, and permitted applications must remain consistent with its certification.
- **Grounded facts:** The BPK record identifies Permen 26/2008 as current and concerned with technical fire-protection systems. A foreign listing guide can explain assembly traceability but cannot confer Indonesian approval. Applied film must not be assumed neutral to a rated door or glazing system.
- **Incorporation:** Teach writers to identify labels, rated components, escape-route function, hardware, visibility, and the approving party before discussing film. Route project approval to the fire-door/glazing manufacturer, responsible designer, authority, and current documentation.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not infer a fire rating, permitted alteration, smoke/egress behavior, or local approval from a marketing sheet or foreign guide. GATE-06 is mandatory.

### KR-04 — Safety glass, impact testing, and intended-use separation

- **Sources:** [SNI 9144-1:2022 laminated safety glass catalog](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/14416-sni9144-12022), [SNI 9144-2:2022 tempered safety glass catalog](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/14417-sni9144-22022), and [ISO 29584:2015 pendulum impact abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/62469.html).
- **Grade:** B for BSN catalog records; C for the ISO abstract.
- **Purpose:** Separate safety-glass material identity, impact classification, intended location, and applied-film claims.
- **Summary:** BSN publishes current catalog records for laminated and tempered safety glass for buildings/panels. ISO 29584 provides a pendulum-impact test and classification framework for safe breakage characteristics, but expressly does not select the intended use or installation location.
- **Grounded facts:** A safety-glass standard title is not evidence that existing door glass conforms. A test classification describes the tested specimen and method. “Safety glass,” “safety film,” fragment retention, and code-appropriate glazing are not interchangeable labels.
- **Incorporation:** Use markings and records as leads, then require traceable glass identity and applicable location rules. Explain that film may have a tested role only when the exact film/glass/edge condition or assembly and relevant classification are evidenced.
- **Limits/recheck:** Public abstracts do not supply clauses or project thresholds. Do not declare an existing door safe, allow continued use after breakage, or claim retrofit film converts unknown glass into compliant safety glazing. Apply GATE-01, GATE-02, and GATE-04.

### KR-05 — Door-glass construction, coatings, and identification

- **Sources:** [SNI 9237:2023 coated glass catalog](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/14858-sni92372023), [SNI 9144-1:2022 catalog](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/14416-sni9144-12022), [LBNL Optics downloads](https://windows.lbl.gov/optics-downloads), and [3M glass checklist](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1705610O/glass-checklist.pdf).
- **Grade:** B for BSN catalog records; C for LBNL; E for the 3M checklist.
- **Purpose:** Establish the substrate-identification fields required before performance or compatibility analysis.
- **Summary:** Applied-film behavior depends on more than pane dimensions. Relevant fields include monolithic/laminated/insulating construction, heat treatment, tint, coating and surface, pane makeup, edge/frame condition, orientation, shading, existing layers, and historical breakage.
- **Grounded facts:** BSN catalog titles distinguish coated and laminated safety-glass product families. LBNL optical tools distinguish material-layer data from constructed glazing-system data. The manufacturer checklist demonstrates that film selection/warranty workflows may require detailed glass construction and site inputs.
- **Incorporation:** Build identification, label-reading, document-recovery, orientation, survey, and photo checklists around observable facts plus uncertainty. Mark unknown fields rather than guessing from color, sound, or appearance.
- **Limits/recheck:** The 3M checklist is product/market-specific and does not approve another film. Visual observation alone cannot prove hidden interlayers, coating surface, heat treatment, or IGU makeup. Apply GATE-02 and GATE-03.

### KR-06 — Film families and disciplined product language

- **Sources:** [IWFA resources](https://iwfa.com/resources/), [3M flat-glass warranty bulletin dated 01 March 2026](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2405554O/3m-window-films-and-3m-ipa-for-flat-glass-applications-warranty-bulletin.pdf), and [3M DIY architectural-film FAQ](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/home-improvement-us/diywindowfilms/).
- **Grade:** D for IWFA; E for 3M.
- **Purpose:** Define film families by intended function while controlling extrapolation from marketing terms.
- **Summary:** Solar-control, decorative/privacy, safety/security, and sacrificial surface-protection films can have different constructions, installation sides, attachment needs, warranties, and performance evidence. Similar-sounding labels do not establish equivalent function.
- **Grounded facts:** Manufacturer documentation separates sun-control, safety/security, anti-graffiti, and attachment products. The exact data sheet, specification, compatible glass, installation side, and market terms control a product claim.
- **Incorporation:** Compare technology families by goal, declared metric, appearance, substrate limits, installation system, test evidence, maintenance, and lifecycle—not by a single marketing number or thickness.
- **Limits/recheck:** The listed 3M products are examples, not recommendations. Do not generalize product availability, service life, warranty, or suitability to Indonesia. Apply GATE-03, GATE-07, and GATE-10.

### KR-07 — Optical, solar, UV, glare, and daylight metrics

- **Sources:** [ISO 9050:2003 abstract and revision status](https://www.iso.org/standard/35062.html), [NFRC residential product certification](https://nfrc.org/residential/), and [LBNL Optics downloads](https://windows.lbl.gov/optics-downloads).
- **Grade:** C.
- **Purpose:** Provide a measurement vocabulary for solar/optical content without promising energy or comfort outcomes.
- **Summary:** Light transmittance, solar-energy behavior, UV transmittance, and related glazing factors are measured properties with defined methods. NFRC and LBNL illustrate that ratings/data belong to specified fenestration products or constructed glazing systems, not to an unqualified film name.
- **Grounded facts:** ISO 9050 covers methods for light and solar-energy transmittance and related glazing factors; the ISO page says the 2003 edition is current but expected to be replaced. Visible transmittance and solar heat-gain behavior describe different outcomes. Installed comfort, glare, fading, and energy effects also depend on orientation, area, lighting, HVAC, occupancy, baseline, and the exact glass-film system.
- **Incorporation:** Define metrics with the source method, compare like-for-like constructions, document baseline and measurement conditions, and use mockups for subjective appearance/glare. State that UV is one contributor to fading, not the only one.
- **Limits/recheck:** NFRC is a U.S. certification context and ISO 9050 is under revision. Do not convert center-of-glass or reference-substrate data into whole-door savings, room-temperature promises, or Indonesian compliance. Apply GATE-07.

### KR-08 — Privacy, manifestation, graphics, and lighting

- **Sources:** [Permen PUPR 14/2017 PDF](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/136346/PermenPUPR14-2017.pdf), [3M daytime privacy product page](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005681000/), [3M window-film simulator](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/building-window-solutions-us/support/window-film-simulator/), and [3M Window Graphic Film bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/997054O/3mtm-window-graphic-film-8170-p50-and-8170-p40.pdf).
- **Grade:** A for the Indonesian accessibility source; E for product examples.
- **Purpose:** Separate collision visibility, privacy, branding, wayfinding, and thermal implications.
- **Summary:** Privacy is directional and lighting-dependent; manifestation is a collision-visibility measure; branding is a communication layer. A single film pattern can affect all three but does not automatically satisfy each goal.
- **Grounded facts:** A product described as providing daytime privacy is not evidence of night privacy. Sightlines differ by user height, direction, and distance. High-opacity graphics and dark printed areas can change absorption, so graphics and old decals also belong in compatibility screening.
- **Incorporation:** Require a two-sided mockup across representative day/night lighting, user sightlines, approach paths, background contrast, door movement, signage hierarchy, and cleaning conditions. Keep the manifestation purpose distinct from glass-strength claims.
- **Limits/recheck:** Product pages demonstrate limitations, not universal optics. Do not promise one-way or 24-hour privacy, prescribe a statutory marking dimension without the current clause, or approve a graphic-film/glass combination remotely. Apply GATE-01, GATE-03, and GATE-07.

### KR-09 — Fragment retention, forced-entry delay, and tested assemblies

- **Sources:** [IWFA safety and security guidance](https://iwfa.com/safety-and-security/), [ISO 16936-1:2020 forced-entry abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/75209.html), [ISO 16936-3:2005 manual-attack abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/38171.html), and [3M safety/security installation bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf).
- **Grade:** C for ISO; D for IWFA; E for 3M.
- **Purpose:** Control safety/security language and anchor it to a defined threat, test, and full glazing assembly.
- **Summary:** Fragment retention, accidental impact, forced-entry delay, blast, windstorm, and ballistic resistance are different problems. Relevant film, attachment, glass, frame, anchors, dimensions, and installation details must match the evidence.
- **Grounded facts:** ISO 16936 parts define particular forced-entry test methods rather than a universal “secure” label. IWFA says threat events need different protocols and installation techniques and states it is unaware of film with ballistic properties. 3M's bulletin says its named films are not bulletproof and not designed to stop intruders; listed mitigation applications require exact specifications and, in some cases, a named attachment system.
- **Incorporation:** Start with threat definition and desired outcome; then map every tested component and deviation. Use “may help retain fragments” or “tested for a specified assembly/condition” only when the supporting report permits that wording.
- **Limits/recheck:** Never promise burglar-proof, bulletproof, a generic delay time, structural capacity, or continued safe door operation after breakage. Marketing demonstrations are not test reports. Apply GATE-04 and GATE-05.

### KR-10 — Film-to-glass compatibility and thermal-stress screening

- **Sources:** [3M thermal-stress bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704947O/thermal-stress.pdf), [3M glass checklist](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1705610O/glass-checklist.pdf), [3M film-to-glass recommendation table](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704864O/glass-recommendation-table.pdf), and [3M graphic-film bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/997054O/3mtm-window-graphic-film-8170-p50-and-8170-p40.pdf).
- **Grade:** E.
- **Purpose:** Turn “can this film go on this door?” into a documented compatibility workflow.
- **Summary:** Thermal risk depends on the exact film and glazing construction plus edge strength, pane size/thickness, coating, tint, IGU makeup, orientation, partial shade, adjacent blinds/curtains, decals, HVAC discharge, and existing damage.
- **Grounded facts:** The 3M bulletin explains thermal stress as a temperature differential between the center and shaded edge and lists partial shading, close window treatments, decals, and directed heating/cooling as contributors. Its checklist captures glass and site variables before selection. Its recommendation table is a product-specific compatibility aid, not a universal rule.
- **Incorporation:** Survey first; escalate unknown glass, damaged edges, prior breakage, coated/laminated/IGU configurations, uneven heating, and mixed graphics. Obtain the selected manufacturer's current written compatibility result for the recorded assembly.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not reuse numeric stress values, old compatibility cells, or one manufacturer's approval for another product. A checklist is not a guarantee. Apply GATE-02 and GATE-03.

### KR-11 — Survey, performance brief, and representative mockup

- **Sources:** [3M glass checklist](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1705610O/glass-checklist.pdf), [3M safety/security installation bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf), and [LBNL Optics downloads](https://windows.lbl.gov/optics-downloads).
- **Grade:** C/E.
- **Purpose:** Define the minimum evidence package before specification, pricing, or installation.
- **Summary:** A useful survey records each door/panel, dimensions, operability, glass and coating clues, frame/gasket/hardware, film side, edge access, defects, exposure, lighting/privacy views, sensors/readers, labels, photos, documents, and unresolved questions. The performance brief then converts user goals into measurable or observable acceptance evidence.
- **Grounded facts:** Manufacturer guidance requires inspection/documentation of glass defects and site conditions before work. Optical data must be tied to a defined construction. A representative mockup must reproduce the critical glass, lighting, view, edge/interface, and installation condition to answer the intended question.
- **Incorporation:** Use panel IDs, photo locations, defect maps, orientation/time observations, sample identifiers, agreed observation conditions, and sign-off responsibilities. Separate the consultation package from the formal installer survey.
- **Limits/recheck:** Remote photos cannot establish hidden construction, edge condition, or system approval. A mockup does not validate unrepresented panels or security/compliance performance. Apply GATE-02, GATE-07, and GATE-08.

### KR-12 — Installation, curing, and workmanship controls

- **Sources:** [3M safety/security installation bulletin, Revision C January 2025](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf), [IWFA architectural installation overview](https://iwfa.com/installation-process/), and [GGF window-film document index](https://www.ggf.org.uk/downloads/window-film/).
- **Grade:** D/E.
- **Purpose:** Support process literacy, site controls, and evidence-based acceptance without turning articles into hazardous DIY instructions.
- **Summary:** Installation quality depends on current product instructions, trained execution, glass/frame condition, a clean work zone, compatible cleaning/application materials, edge detail, water extraction, attachment requirements, curing conditions, and documented pre-existing defects.
- **Grounded facts:** 3M's named-product bulletin requires current technical documents, inspection of glass, written documentation of uncorrected defects, clean/dust-controlled conditions, and product-specific installation. It also acknowledges that some edge contamination or exterior optical limitations can be unavoidable in stated conditions. GGF lists industry documents on installation and visual quality, but full text access is gated.
- **Incorporation:** Explain sequence and hold points; put actual solution ratios, clearances, tools, attachment details, cure/cleaning periods, and visual tolerances in the selected product's current method statement. Coordinate occupied-area access and door isolation with the responsible site team.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not transplant 3M dimensions or cure periods to another film, allow blade work on uncertain coated glass, or give powered-door/fire-door DIY instructions. Obtain current product instructions and acceptance criteria under GATE-08.

### KR-13 — Frames, hardware, automatic-door sensors, and moving edges

- **Sources:** [Hotron SSR-3-ER installation instruction](https://hotron.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/SSR-3-ER-installation-instruction-MP-10362_0104.pdf), [record all-glass automatic sliding-door system](https://www.recorddoors.com/ca/en/products/sliding-doors/all-glass/5200), and [3M safety/security installation bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf).
- **Grade:** E.
- **Purpose:** Establish interface checks for doors that move, sense users, lock, close, or carry edge attachment.
- **Summary:** Film can introduce new edge thickness, overlap, sealant, appearance, or optical reflectance near gaskets, brush seals, patch fittings, tracks, readers, and sensors. Automatic doors are powered safety systems whose detection and reversal behavior must be tested by qualified parties after an optical retrofit.
- **Grounded facts:** The Hotron example uses active infrared/radar sensing with installation and reflection diagnostics. The record example identifies safety sensors and automatic reversal as system functions. Security-film guidance treats the glass/frame edge and attachment as performance-relevant.
- **Incorporation:** Record hardware and sensor make/model, detection fields, film/graphics location, moving clearances, existing faults, access-control function, and pre/post tests. Keep work on electrical controls, automatic operators, closers, locks, and fire interfaces with qualified specialists.
- **Limits/recheck:** The examples do not prove that a particular film will disrupt a sensor, nor that passing one walk test certifies the door. Do not adjust or bypass safety devices. Apply GATE-02 and GATE-09.

### KR-14 — Optical defects, normal cure, and diagnosis evidence

- **Sources:** [3M safety/security installation bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf), [3M flat-glass warranty bulletin dated 01 March 2026](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2405554O/3m-window-films-and-3m-ipa-for-flat-glass-applications-warranty-bulletin.pdf), and [GGF window-film document index](https://www.ggf.org.uk/downloads/window-film/).
- **Grade:** D/E.
- **Purpose:** Distinguish observation, likely cause, acceptance, and warranty entitlement.
- **Summary:** Water pockets, haze, contamination, fibers, scratches, creases, color variation, edge gaps, lifting, and optical distortion need time-stamped observations under agreed lighting, angle, distance, and cure conditions. A symptom label alone does not establish cause or remedy.
- **Grounded facts:** 3M installation guidance lists product-, contamination-, application-, and substrate-related visible conditions. Its 2026 warranty bulletin uses product-specific observation and cure conditions and warns that environmental conditions affect cure time. GGF identifies a separate industry visual-quality guidance document, confirming that acceptance needs a defined method.
- **Incorporation:** Compare against the pre-work defect map and approved mockup; record panel ID, date, lighting, view, scale, edge location, door use, and product cure guidance. Escalate breakage, edge damage, delamination, impaired visibility, or door-function concerns promptly.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not invent a universal viewing distance, particle count, bubble tolerance, or cure deadline. Do not diagnose thermal stress from a photo alone or decide warranty coverage. Apply GATE-08 and GATE-10.

### KR-15 — Cleaning, removal, renewal, warranty, and traceability

- **Sources:** [3M flat-glass warranty bulletin dated 01 March 2026](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2405554O/3m-window-films-and-3m-ipa-for-flat-glass-applications-warranty-bulletin.pdf), [IWFA what to expect](https://iwfa.com/what-to-expect/), and [3M Singapore building-window-film care/warranty page](https://engage.3m.com/bwf-window).
- **Grade:** D/E.
- **Purpose:** Ground lifecycle care and procurement records while preventing invented warranty/service-life claims.
- **Summary:** Care, cure, inspection, removal, adhesive residue, authorized labor, and warranty periods vary by product, application, market, installer, registration, and documentation. Removal can expose uncertain coatings or leave residue and therefore needs a controlled method.
- **Grounded facts:** The 2026 3M bulletin ties coverage to exact products, glass recommendations, inspections, authorized dealers, registration, certificate/address, proof of purchase, care, and specified attachment. It states film is removable but adhesive residue is expected and recommends professional removal for the named system. Regional terms can differ where a local 3M entity publishes them.
- **Incorporation:** Handover product/batch and panel schedules, installation date, installer, data sheet, method, sample/mockup approval, compatibility evidence, test reports, warranty/certificate, cleaning instructions, inspection log, and defect/removal history.
- **Limits/recheck:** The cited terms are manufacturer/market examples, not Indonesian legal advice or guaranteed local coverage. Never publish generic warranty years, lifespan, price, or legal interpretation. Apply GATE-10.

### KR-16 — Evidence hierarchy, QA, handover, and compliance review

- **Sources:** [PP No. 16 Tahun 2021 — BPK](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Details/161846/pp-no-16-tahun-2021), [ISO 29584:2015 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/62469.html), [ISO 16936-1:2020 abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/75209.html), [UL fire-rated door guide](https://www.ul.com/thecodeauthority/knowledge/ul-fire-rated-doors-guide), and [3M safety/security installation bulletin](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1704892O/security-window-film.pdf).
- **Grade:** A/C/E.
- **Purpose:** Give every topic a common verification ladder from claim to installed evidence.
- **Summary:** Evidence strength rises when the question, current governing requirement, standard/test edition, tested specimen, selected product, surveyed assembly, installation record, functional test, acceptance record, and handover file all align.
- **Grounded facts:** A standard abstract defines scope but not project compliance. A test report is relevant only to the tested threat and configuration. A product data sheet is not an installation record. A label or visual marking is a lead until traced. A completed checklist can document inputs without guaranteeing approval.
- **Incorporation:** For every material claim record: claimant; exact wording; source owner/date/version; standard/test; product and batch; glass/panel; frame/attachment; dimensions; deviations; installer; inspection; functional test; approving party; and unresolved gate. Prefer current primary documents over summaries.
- **Limits/recheck:** Do not declare regulatory compliance, safety, security, fire-rating preservation, sensor acceptance, compatibility, or warranty entitlement from an incomplete chain. Use the explicit gates below.

## Explicit evidence gates

| Gate | Release condition | Claims held until released |
|---|---|---|
| `GATE-01` | Current full Indonesian regulation/SNI text, applicable local/project rule, exact clause/edition, and responsible reviewer are identified. | Legal requirement, statutory dimension, mandatory class, or Indonesian compliance. |
